Your phone runs WireGuard, connects through chilhuacle to the OpenWrt One, and you open `http://192.168.1.138/continuity`. The door stays open for 7 days at a time. Nothing of it is built yet. I checked chilhuacle (read-only) and found two things that affect it:

- **Your account has sudo**, which answers Q13.
- **The kernel is too old for built-in WireGuard.** chilhuacle runs Debian 9 with kernel 4.9, and WireGuard only went into the kernel in 5.6. It can still work: `/dev/net/tun` is there, so the userspace version (`wireguard-go`, a single binary) would run. It's just less standard.
- **Disk space is low:** 420 MB free, 96% full. That's less than the ~1 GB we had before.

## Two ways to do it

**A. As designed: your own WireGuard relay through chilhuacle.** This is the full design.
- No outside company is involved, and chilhuacle only passes along traffic it can't read.
- Later, the doorbell and Cleotilde will open and close the door.
- What it needs:
  - `wireguard-go` and the iptables rules on chilhuacle.
  - Two GCP firewall rules (UDP 51820 and 51821), which you add in the console.
  - My SSH key on the One, or you run the commands there.
  - The WireGuard app on your phone (Q15).
- For a first test, you'd open the gate by hand; the doorbell control comes later.
- Realistically this takes an afternoon of work together.

**B. Quick: Tailscale on the Cleotilde Pi.**
- The Pi connects out to Tailscale, so the carrier NAT doesn't matter. It shares only `192.168.1.138` with your phone, and a Tailscale access rule can limit that to port 80.
- No changes to chilhuacle, the router or GCP. It takes about 20 minutes, and Continuity would work on 4G today.
- The downsides: it depends on Tailscale's servers, and it skips the doorbell gate (it's always open unless Cleotilde switches it off).
- You could replace it with A later.

The same caveat applies to both: Continuity has no login, so whoever holds the phone's key can reach all of FACTORY2 port 80. That includes the Factory system, which shares the same Tomcat.

**What needs fixing before you can sell it (10.9):**
- **Shared chilhuacle login.** Every Pi would log into chilhuacle as `pi` with the same key, so one customer could read another customer's Mailbox and pulses. Each unit needs its own login, limited to its own folder.
- **The ring handler only knows Cleotilde.** The name is fixed in the code (`RingDoorbellProcessingHandler.TELEONOME = "Cleotilde"`), so a second unit can't be rung.
- **Zhinu has no HTTPS and no login.** The code crosses 4G unencrypted, and anyone can see every Mailbox page.
- **One relay for all customers.** If chilhuacle goes down, every customer loses access, and its disk is nearly full. The cost of the traffic also has to be planned.
- **Secrets per unit.** Every unit needs its own TOTP secret, WireGuard keys and router passwords, never copies of yours.
- **The ESP32 has no task watchdog.**
